Access Technical Information for the Right Product Choice

When selecting labels, RFID, ribbons and printers, technical specifications matter as much as the general product description. Material construction, adhesive type, temperature limits, print compatibility and usage conditions enable suitability for the application to be assessed.

In this section, you can review product datasheets, catalogues and printer documents under the relevant product groups. Using the product code, you can access technical information, compare alternatives and identify the properties to be evaluated for your application.

Technical documents are the primary sources for product selection. Final suitability, however, is determined by comparing the conditions stated in the document with the actual application and, where necessary, verifying them with sample tests.

1Label Datasheets

Material, Adhesive and Usage Properties

Label datasheets bring together the product's layer structure, physical properties, application conditions and printing information. Depending on the product, they include information such as face material, thickness, grammage, adhesive system, liner and surface coating.

For self-adhesive labels, adhesion to the surface and application temperature are important; for hang tags, mechanical strength, hole structure and fixing method become important. For special conditions such as high temperature, cryogenic storage, outdoor use or chemical contact, the technical limits of the relevant product should be examined separately.

Information to Check When Reviewing the Datasheet

  • Product code and description: Checking that the material being evaluated is the correct product.
  • Face stock and coating: The material's structure, appearance and printability.
  • Thickness and grammage: Distinction between layer values and the total structure.
  • Adhesive properties: Adhesive type, target surfaces and test results, if available.
  • Temperature information: Application temperature, service range and time-dependent peak temperature.
  • Printing recommendations: Suitable printing method and recommended consumables.
  • Resistance information: Chemical, mechanical or environmental performance under the specified conditions.
  • Storage conditions: Storage before use and shelf-life information, if applicable.

Not every property may be defined for all products. A requirement not included in the datasheet should not be interpreted as meaning the product is suitable for that condition.

2RFID Datasheets

Evaluate the Chip, Antenna and Final Label Construction Together

RFID datasheets provide information on the chip, antenna, frequency, protocol, memory and physical construction. An inlay document and the datasheet of a finished RFID label may describe different scopes.

While the inlay datasheet focuses on the electronic structure, the final label documentation also considers the face material, adhesive, dimensions, protective layers and application conditions. Therefore, matching only the chip model or antenna size does not mean that two RFID labels will deliver the same performance.

Information to Check When Reviewing the Datasheet

  • Chip model and memory: EPC, TID and, where available, user memory specifications.
  • Frequency and protocol: Compatibility with the reader to be used and the target market.
  • Antenna, inlay and label dimensions: Correct interpretation of different physical dimensions.
  • Supply format: Dry inlay, wet inlay or finished label.
  • Target surface: Metal, plastic, cardboard or special application details.
  • Temperature limits: Distinction between operating, storage and, where applicable, process exposure.
  • Performance information: Measurement frequency, power, orientation, surface and other test conditions.
  • Approval details: The relevant application specification and approval scope, if any.

The read range in the datasheet is a reference for defined measurement conditions. It should not be assumed that the same result will be achieved with the actual product, packaging format and reader infrastructure.

3Ribbon Datasheets

Ribbon Selection to Suit the Print Surface and Durability Requirements

Ribbon datasheets describe the product's compatibility with printing technology, recommended label surfaces and information on print durability.

Classification as wax, wax/resin or resin simplifies the initial assessment. However, ribbons in the same group may have different properties in terms of print energy, speed, surface compatibility and chemical resistance.

When selecting a codeLINE® ribbon, the properties of the relevant product code should be assessed together with the label and printer to be used.

Information to Check When Reviewing the Datasheet

  • Ribbon type: Wax, wax/resin, resin or application-specific formulation.
  • Head technology: Flat-head or near-edge compatibility.
  • Recommended surfaces: Paper, synthetic film or specially coated materials.
  • Printing conditions: Recommended speed and energy settings, if specified.
  • Resistance properties: Test results for abrasion, temperature and specific chemicals.
  • Physical fit: Order specifications for width, length, core and ink side.
  • Storage conditions: Storage requirements for the ribbon prior to use.

When reviewing resistance results, check on which label surface and under which conditions the print was tested. The same result cannot be considered guaranteed when switching to a different surface.

In high-temperature applications, the resistance limit of the label and the durability of the ribbon print should be assessed separately.

4Printer Technical Documents

Printer Selection, Media Compatibility and Usage Information

Printer technical documents describe the device's print capacity, supported materials, connectivity options and usage requirements.

While the product brochure introduces the general features, the user manual details procedures such as media loading, sensor adjustment, calibration, cleaning and troubleshooting. Both documents should be taken into account when selecting consumables.

Information to Check in the Documents

  • Printing method: Direct thermal, thermal transfer or inkjet.
  • Print width and resolution: Compatibility with label design and information density.
  • Media specifications: Supported width, thickness, roll diameter and core size.
  • Sensing method: Support for label gap, black mark or continuous media.
  • Consumable requirements: Compatible ribbon, ink and media specifications.
  • Connectivity and software: Communication options with the system to be used.
  • Optional hardware: Cutter, peeler, rewinder or RFID encoding options.
  • Maintenance and use: Cleaning, operating environment and model-specific instructions.

For RFID printers, the encoder's frequency and protocol support should also be checked. It should not be assumed that all printers in the same series have the same hardware.

5Product Catalogues

Review Product Families and Alternatives Together

Product catalogues present different label, RFID, ribbon and printer groups together with their general areas of use. They are used to identify the product family that suits your needs and to compare alternatives at an early stage.

Catalogues and product datasheets complement each other. While the catalogue shows the product range, the datasheet details the properties and conditions of use of a specific product code.

When Using the Catalogues

First identify the product group suited to your application. Then review the datasheets of candidate products and compare surface, temperature, printing and durability requirements.

General descriptions in the catalogue do not mean that every product has the same property. Temperature, chemical resistance and specific approval information in particular must be verified for each product code.

6Frequently Asked Questions

What Is the Difference Between a Datasheet and a Catalogue?

The catalogue gives a general introduction to product families and application areas. A datasheet, by contrast, describes the construction, properties and defined conditions of use of a specific product.

Do the Values in the Datasheet Apply to Every Application?

Values are assessed together with the specified test methods and conditions. A result on a standard test surface does not by itself indicate that the same value will be achieved on the actual product surface.

What Does “Typical Value” Mean?

A typical value indicates the general character of the product under the specified measurement conditions. It should not be used in the same sense as a minimum acceptance limit or a guaranteed specification value. The scope of the value should be checked against the explanation in the document.

Is a Label's Maximum Temperature Rating a Sufficient Selection Criterion?

No. Dwell time at temperature, number of cycles, application method and print legibility should also be assessed. Short-term peak temperature and the continuous-use limit may differ.

Does an Approval or Declaration of Conformity Cover All Products?

Check which product code, construction, test or usage condition the document applies to. A document for a specific option within a product family should not be assumed to apply to all other options.

What Should I Do If the Technical Information I Need Is Not in the Datasheet?

You can share the product code and the property you need with our technical team. Your requirement is assessed on the basis of available documents and application conditions.

Is a Sample Trial Needed After Reviewing the Datasheet?

Sample verification is important for new surfaces, special processes and harsh operating conditions. Testing helps assess the label's adhesion to the actual surface, print durability and, where required, RFID performance.

Which Document Version Should I Use?

The current revision matching the product code should be used as the reference. Recording the date and revision information of the document used in sample and series production approvals makes technical tracking easier.

When Comparing Technical Documents

To compare products reliably, numerical values alone should not be considered. The unit, test method, measurement surface, dwell time and environmental conditions must also match.

It should be checked whether the thickness value refers to a single layer or the total construction, whether the temperature value defines application or service conditions, and with which print combination the resistance result was obtained.

The absence of a value in the document does not mean that property is unlimited. Critical requirements must be clearly defined during the technical assessment.
