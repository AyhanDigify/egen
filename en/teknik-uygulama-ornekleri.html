Technical Information

Application Examples

Label Performance Is Determined by the Conditions of Use

A label's technical specifications become meaningful in conjunction with the surface it will be applied to and the operating conditions. The same material may give different results on a smooth metal surface, an oily cast part or cold packaging. Therefore, correctly defining the labelling requirement is the first step in choosing a suitable product.

In this section, we address high-temperature, difficult-surface, outdoor, cryogenic storage, equipment tracking, packaging security and print cover-up applications from a technical perspective. For each application, we explain how the material, adhesive, print and fixing method should be evaluated.

The examples below have been prepared to illustrate the technical selection process. Final suitability is determined by verifying the selected label construction on the actual product surface and under the defined usage conditions.

1High-Temperature Labelling

Application: Identification of a Heat-Treated Metal Part

In heat treatment, hot rolling or furnace processes, product identification must remain readable after processing. Due to the temperature, the label may undergo dimensional change, its surface coating may be damaged, it may lose adhesion or its print may become illegible.

When making a selection, it should first be determined whether the label will be applied to a hot surface, heated together with the product, or used after the process. These scenarios require different materials and adhesive systems.

What Is Assessed Technically?

  • The actual temperature and exposure time at the point where the label is applied.
  • Heating-cooling cycles and how many times the process is repeated.
  • Presence of mill scale, oil, coating or an oxide layer on the surface.
  • Fixing the label by adhesion or mechanical attachment.
  • Whether the barcode will be read while hot or after cooling.
  • Process resistance of the ribbon or other marking methods used.

The oven's set temperature may not match the actual temperature of the label. A short-term peak temperature should also not be regarded as the continuous use limit.

How Is the Label Construction Determined?

Depending on process conditions, polyester, polyimide, aluminium, steel or composite constructions are considered. Where direct adhesion is not suitable, a hang tag and mechanical fastening may be preferred. For heat-activated labels, the surface temperature required for application is also taken into account.

When selecting a print method, the durability of the material and the durability of the print are considered separately. Thermal transfer or, for suitable products, laser marking options should be verified based on the result after the process.

Properties to verify on the sample: material integrity, edge lifting, bond strength, coating condition and barcode readability.

2Rough and Difficult Surfaces

Application: Applying Labels to Oily or Rough Metal Surfaces

On castings, steel coils, painted metals, rubber and low-surface-energy plastics, standard adhesives may not provide sufficient contact. Even if the label appears to adhere initially, it may detach after transport, temperature changes or surface movement.

On rough surfaces, the area the adhesive can contact is reduced. Oil, dust, loose rust or coatings detaching from the surface can form a weak intermediate layer between the adhesive and the product.

What Is Assessed Technically?

The surface material, roughness, temperature and contamination should be defined. In oily applications, the type and amount of oil and whether it forms a mobile film on the surface are important.

The flexibility of the label material and the adhesive coat weight also affect contact behaviour. Using more adhesive does not mean higher performance on every surface.

How Is the Label Construction Determined?

A strong adhesive suited to the surface, a face material with sufficient flexibility and an appropriate label size are selected together. On small-diameter surfaces, the material's tendency to return to a flat form can cause edge lifting, so thickness and rigidity are also assessed.

It is important to remove loose surface layers as far as possible, use the product at the recommended application temperature and apply sufficient application pressure.

Properties to be verified on samples: Initial tack, adhesion after dwell time, edge lifting and performance under actual contamination conditions. Trials should also be repeated on surfaces taken from different production batches.

3Outdoor Labelling

Application: Long-Term Identification of Outdoor Equipment

Outdoor labels may be exposed to sunlight, rain, condensation, temperature changes and mechanical abrasion simultaneously. A seaside location, a site with intense sunlight and regularly washed equipment do not share the same conditions of use.

In these applications, it may not be enough for the label simply to stay on the surface. The product information, warnings, serial number and barcode must remain legible for the intended period.

What Is Assessed Technically?

  • Expected service life and climatic conditions.
  • Direction and duration of exposure to sunlight.
  • Continuous wetness, saline environments or pressure washing.
  • Contact with cleaning products, fuel and oil.
  • Exposure of the label to handling and abrasion.
  • Coating of the application surface and temperature changes.

How Is the Label Construction Determined?

The face material, adhesive and printing system are selected together. Protective lamination is considered where necessary; however, the lamination must also be suitable for UV, temperature and chemical conditions.

As clear or glossy protective layers may affect optical barcode reading, the final construction should be checked at actual reading angles.

Properties to be verified on the sample: Adhesion, discolouration, cracking, edge lifting, print abrasion and readability. Accelerated ageing results should not be interpreted directly as a service life in years without being correlated with the conditions of use.

4Cold Environment and Cryogenic Applications

Application: Labelling a Sample Tube Stored in a Freezer

Cold storage, deep freezing and cryogenic storage have different technical requirements. Applying a label at room temperature and cooling it afterwards is not the same application as adhering it to a pre-cooled surface.

Condensation or ice on the surface can limit the adhesive's contact with the product. On small-diameter tubes, the rigidity of the material, the length of the label and the way it is wrapped affect edge lifting.

What Is Assessed Technically?

The application temperature, storage temperature, storage period and freeze–thaw cycles must be determined. The material, diameter and surface properties of the sample container should also be recorded.

In applications using liquid nitrogen, liquid-phase and vapour-phase conditions must be distinguished. The technical documentation should be checked to see which form of exposure the relevant product has been evaluated for.

How Is the Label Construction Determined?

A combination of an adhesive developed for low temperatures, a suitable film and durable printing is selected. If a dwell time before chilling is required for the necessary adhesion build-up, this time is included in the application plan.

The label size is determined so that it does not obstruct the tube, cap or area of use and ensures the information remains legible.

Properties to be verified on samples: Adhesion after chilling, edge lifting during thawing, print resistance under condensation and legibility of identification information.

5Asset and Equipment Tracking

Application: Identifying Machinery, Hand Tools or Handling Equipment

Asset labels link equipment to its registration, maintenance, assignment and inventory information. Application can be done with a printed serial number, and can also be supported by barcode, Data Matrix, QR code or RFID.

Before selection, it should be determined how the equipment will be identified and at which point it will be read. Individual line-of-sight scanning and bulk or contactless RFID reading require different technical constructions.

What Is Assessed Technically?

Whether the surface is metal, plastic or painted, and whether the equipment moves, is washed, is used outdoors or comes into contact with chemicals, are taken into account. The label position should be chosen in an area where it will not wear during use and can be read.

In RFID applications, the influence of metal, reader layout, antenna orientation and other nearby tags can alter performance. Read range should not be treated on its own as a fixed product value.

How Is the Label Construction Determined?

For long-term visual identification, a durable film and a suitable adhesive are used; for RFID on metal surfaces, an on-metal construction is considered. Under heavy mechanical conditions, RFID products with suitable housings or mechanical fixing options can be considered.

The printed identity must correctly match the data written to the RFID chip. The label should be used linked to the information in the records system.

Properties to be verified on the sample: Surface adhesion, resistance to abrasion and cleaning, data matching and read performance in the actual operating position.

6Packaging Security

Application: Making Package Opening or Label Tampering Visible

Security labels are used to provide a visible indication of removal or package opening. VOID messages, fragmenting films or fibre-tearing paper constructions behave differently on different surfaces.

For the label to work effectively, it must be placed correctly over the opening point of the package. A label that does not cross the opening line or lacks a suitable contact area may not provide the expected tamper indication.

What Is Assessed Technically?

  • The packaging material, coating and surface energy.
  • Opening direction and the label's contact area on the lid and body.
  • Time required for adhesion to build up after application.
  • Peel speed, angle and ambient temperature.
  • Expectation of leaving a visible trace or leaving no residue on the surface.

How Is the Label Construction Determined?

VOID constructions can leave a message on the label and/or the surface when removed. Frangible labels break apart on removal, while fibre-tearing constructions are assessed on the basis that, together with a suitable packaging surface, they produce visible damage.

A security label is an element that makes tampering visible. On its own, it does not guarantee the authenticity of the product or that the packaging cannot be opened by any method.

Properties to be verified on the sample: formation of the tamper evidence after the defined dwell time, visibility of the evidence and consistency across different packaging batches.

7Price and Print Covering

Application: Covering a Pre-Printed Price or Barcode

Label constructions with high opacity are considered when a price change, a product information update or covering of old print on the packaging is required.

A standard white label appearing white does not mean it will completely cover the dark print beneath it. If an old barcode or text shows through the label, it may affect how the new information is perceived and read.

What Is Assessed Technically?

The colour, density and size of the print to be covered should be determined together with the size and print area of the new label. The label should be positioned so that the old information is not left exposed at the edges.

If the label is to be applied to a previously labelled surface, check that the new adhesive bonds to the coating of the old label and that the layers stay on the surface together.

How Is the Label Construction Determined?

Constructions with a higher-opacity face material or a suitable backing layer are selected. The background of the new barcode and the surrounding blank areas should be designed so that they are not affected by the underlying print.

It should also be determined whether the new label is to be temporary or permanent. If removability is expected, whether removal damages the old print or the packaging should be tested separately.

Properties to verify on the sample: Visibility of the old print, contrast of the new print, label position and barcode quality. A successful scan with a single reader does not on its own show that all barcode quality requirements are met.

08How Should Application Trials Be Planned?

Before sample trials, the criteria by which success will be assessed should be defined. The statement “the label stayed in place” does not on its own satisfy other requirements such as print, legibility and service life.

In a sound evaluation:

  • Initial conditions are recorded: Actual surface, cleanliness, application temperature and labelling method.
  • The label structure is defined: Product code, size, print consumable, printer settings and connection method.
  • The actual process is applied: Temperature, chemical contact, washing, abrasion or handling conditions.
  • Results are compared: Adhesion, material integrity, print condition, barcode and, where required, RFID performance.
  • The usage decision is recorded: The approved construction and its limits serve as the reference for series application.

When a significant change is made to the surface, print consumables or process conditions, it should be reassessed whether the previous sample approval covers the new conditions.
