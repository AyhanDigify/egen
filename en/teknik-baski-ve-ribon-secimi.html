Technical Information

Print and Ribbon Selection

Durable, Legible Printing Starts with Compatible Materials

The legibility of the barcode, serial number, product information and technical descriptions on the label depends on the label surface, the printing consumable and the printer working together. An unsuitable combination can cause faint printing, smearing, barcode reading errors, ribbon wrinkling and production losses.

When choosing the right printing system, it is not only the appearance of the initial print that is assessed. Factors such as the label's service life and its exposure to temperature, humidity, chemical contact and abrasion are also taken into account. A print that looks sharp at first may not maintain the same performance under real operating conditions.

Egen Barkod assesses the label material, ribbon or ink, printer technology and application conditions together. In this section we explain direct thermal, thermal transfer and inkjet printing methods, ribbon types, technical compatibility criteria and common printing problems.

1Direct Thermal and Thermal Transfer

How Does Direct Thermal Printing Work?

In direct thermal printing, the image is formed on the heat-sensitive surface of the label by the heat applied by the printhead. No separate ribbon is used. For this reason, the label material must be suitable for direct thermal printing.

They can be considered for shipping, pricing, weighing and limited-duration product identification applications. However, suitability depends on how long the print must be preserved and on environmental conditions.

Heat, light, abrasion, oil and certain chemicals can affect the thermal image. The performance of top-coated and uncoated thermal materials also differs. Therefore, not all thermal labels should be regarded as having the same level of durability.

How Does Thermal Transfer Printing Work?

In thermal transfer printing, the printhead transfers the ink on the ribbon onto the label surface. The image is formed not by a thermal reaction in the label itself, but by the transfer of the print layer on the ribbon.

It can be used on paper, synthetic film and suitably coated special materials. Print adhesion and durability depend on the compatibility of the ribbon formulation, label coating and printer settings.

Suitable thermal transfer combinations are assessed for long-term product identification, industrial barcodes, equipment identification and demanding process applications.

Thermal transfer printing can provide high durability with the right combination; however, it should not be assumed that printing with every ribbon is resistant to heat or chemicals.

2Choosing Wax, Wax/Resin and Resin Ribbons

Wax Ribbons

Wax ribbons are mainly considered for standard labelling needs on paper surfaces. When matched with a suitable material, they can help achieve a sharp image at low print energy.

It can be used in applications such as cartons, shelves, shipping and general product identification. Where heavy abrasion, high temperatures or chemical contact are involved, resistance should be checked separately.

When selecting a wax ribbon, the paper's coating, roughness and print speed are taken into account. The same ribbon may give different print results on different paper surfaces.

Wax/Resin Ribbons

Wax/resin ribbons are considered for coated papers and suitable synthetic surfaces, depending on print quality and durability expectations.

Depending on the product formulation, it can offer higher rub and smudge resistance than standard wax options. However, the wax/resin group does not denote a single level of durability.

For glossy surfaces, heavy handling and certain environmental effects, the ribbon datasheet should be reviewed; adhesion to the surface and in-use performance should be verified on a sample.

Resin Ribbons

Resin ribbons are considered for applications requiring high print durability on suitable synthetic films and specially coated labels.

Industrial product identification, electronic components, polyester and polyimide labels, and applications exposed to certain chemical or temperature effects are examples of this group.

However, the term resin does not mean resistance to all solvents or to all high-temperature processes. There can be significant differences between resin products in terms of print energy, surface compatibility, chemical resistance and process performance.

codeLINE® Ribbon Options

Ribbons are prepared with width, length and winding specifications suited to printer requirements.

Product selection is not based solely on the wax, wax/resin or resin classification. The label face stock, printhead technology, print speed and expected durability are assessed together.

Ribbon Selection for High-Temperature Printing

In high-temperature applications, preserving the print after the process is as important as the label's base material.

CL 738 and CL 739 are our high-temperature ribbon options used with the PMT 7060, APT 7100, HCT 7090, STT 8000 and SCL 6075 products. The appropriate ribbon is selected according to the label surface, temperature profile, exposure time and post-process legibility expectations.

The label material withstanding the specified maximum temperature does not indicate that the ribbon print will also remain unchanged at the same temperature. The final combination must be validated under actual process conditions.

3Label, Ribbon and Printer Compatibility

Selecting the Print Surface and Ribbon Together

In thermal transfer printing, the layer to which the ink adheres is often the print coating on the material. Two labels using the same PET or polyimide base may give different results with the same ribbon due to different surface coatings.

Pre-printing, varnish or protective coatings can also affect subsequent thermal transfer printing. The area where variable information will be printed should be prepared to suit the ribbon to be used.

Flat-Head and Near-Edge Technologies

Thermal transfer printers use different printhead technologies such as flat-head and near-edge. The ribbon must suit the printhead design and the ink's release behaviour after transfer.

For this reason, two ribbons of the same width and length may not be interchangeable across different printer technologies. When making a selection for a barcode printer or packaging coding system, the technical requirements of the device should be taken as the basis.

Physical Properties of Ribbon

The following information should be checked when ordering ribbon:

  • Width: Must suit the material to be printed and the printer’s operating instructions.
  • Length and outer diameter: Must not exceed the printer's capacity.
  • Core size and connection type: Must suit the ribbon spindle.
  • Ink orientation: Ink-in or ink-out winding should be selected as required by the printer.
  • Head technology: The ribbon's flat-head or near-edge compatibility must be verified.

When determining ribbon width, the width of the text to be printed should not be the only consideration. The printer manufacturer's recommendations on media and printhead protection should also be followed.

Print Energy, Speed and Pressure

Print energy affects the transfer of ink from the ribbon to the surface. Insufficient energy can cause faint or incomplete printing, while excessive energy can cause line spreading, filled-in characters and ribbon damage.

As print speed increases, the time available for transfer changes. Speed and energy settings should therefore be assessed together. On printers with adjustable pressure, the balance of printhead pressure also affects consistency throughout printing.

The aim is to find the right setting that delivers the required print quality. Darker printing does not always mean a higher-quality barcode.

Resolution and Barcode Design

Printer resolution matters for small characters, fine lines and data-dense codes. However, high resolution alone does not correct poor design or incompatible consumables.

The module size of the barcode, the quiet zones around it, its contrast and print direction must be taken into account. Preparing the design to suit the printer's dot structure helps ensure that bars and cells are formed consistently.

4Inkjet Label and Printer Compatibility

The Role of the Surface Coating on Inkjet Labels

In inkjet printing, the label's surface coating affects ink droplet spread, drying behaviour, colour appearance and adhesion to the surface.

An unsuitable surface can lead to problems such as ink spreading, colour bleeding, slow drying or easy smudging. For this reason, a label suitable for thermal transfer printing should not automatically be considered suitable for inkjet printing.

Dye and Pigment Ink Compatibility

Dye and pigment inks have different structures. The compatibility of the label coating with the ink technology of the printer to be used should be checked.

Ink type alone does not determine water, light or chemical resistance. The result depends on the combination of ink, coating, base material and conditions of use.

Matte, Gloss and Clear Labels

Matte and gloss surfaces produce different results in terms of colour appearance and reflection. With transparent labels, the colour of the packaging can affect the appearance of the printed area and the barcode contrast.

On a printer without white ink capability, white areas in the design remain unprinted. On transparent material, the underlying surface shows through these areas. The design should be assessed on the actual packaging.

Drying, Colour Adjustment and Mechanical Compatibility

The media profile, print mode and ink density affect colour accuracy and drying time. If the labels are to be rewound, subjected to rubbing or applied immediately after printing, the required drying conditions must be verified.

Roll diameter, core size, label gap, liner and sensor properties must also be compatible with the printer. For double-sided materials or materials of special thickness, feeding and print suitability are checked separately.

Inkjet Labels with RFID

For labels containing an RFID inlay, the thickness of the chip area, the label geometry and the printer feed are assessed together.

Colour printing on the label and writing data to the RFID chip are different processes. Encoding requirements should be addressed separately with a suitable RFID encoding system and data plan.

5Printing Problems and Solutions

How to Identify the Source of the Problem

Print problems may stem from material incompatibility, incorrect settings, loading errors, contamination or mechanical wear. Before resolving the problem, the label, ribbon, printer model and settings used should be recorded.

Where possible, change only one parameter per trial and observe its effect on the result.

These symptoms are not specific to a single cause. Mechanical adjustments and cleaning should be carried out according to the printer's user manual.

Printhead and Feed Maintenance

Label dust, adhesive residue and ribbon deposits can affect print quality. The printhead, platen roller and sensors should be cleaned using the methods and materials recommended by the manufacturer.

Attempting to print with excessive energy or pressure does not permanently resolve a material incompatibility. Correct consumable selection and regular maintenance should be considered together.

How to Verify Print Quality and Durability?

A visually clean first print does not on its own demonstrate suitability for use. Two separate aspects should be addressed when evaluating samples:

Print quality: Character integrity, barcode contrast, symbol dimensions, quiet zones and data accuracy.

Print durability: Retention of information after abrasion, temperature, humidity, chemical contact or washing during use.

A barcode being readable by a single scanner is not the same as standards-based print quality verification. In critical applications, measurements should be taken with a suitable barcode verifier.

Test records should include the label and ribbon codes, printer model, resolution, speed, energy setting and the test conditions applied. This information should be used as a reference in series use; the effect of any material or process changes should be re-evaluated.
